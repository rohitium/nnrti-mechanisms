## Replacement

**Find:**

> The hydrogen bond between DOR and Lys103 backbone is indifferent to what happens to the side chain at that position. K103N shortens the side chain and pulls its polar atoms more than 3 Å closer to the drug, yet the main-chain hydrogen bond is unchanged, and it survives intact in all four K103N-containing genotypes (Table 3). This is the structural expression of the design intent behind DOR:14,15 by engaging the backbone rather than the Lys103 side chain, the drug sidesteps the mechanism by which K103N resists EFV and NVP — stabilization of the closed, unliganded pocket that slows inhibitor entry9 and permits catalysis with inhibitor bound.8 It may in part explain why K103N+M230L and L100I+K103N also show insignificant binding penalties in our simulations (∆∆Gbind = 0.59 ± 0.41 and 0.19 ± 0.68 kcal/mol).

**Replace:**

> K103N does not confer resistance by removing a contact. It stabilizes the closed, unliganded form of the NNIBP and thereby slows inhibitor entry,9 and it allows a catalytically productive RT–template/primer–dNTP complex to assemble even with EFV still bound.8 The substitution acts on the pocket rather than on any particular interaction. ETR and RPV counter this by adaptation: their torsional freedom lets the bound inhibitor reorient and reposition within an altered pocket, preserving productive contacts across a range of NNIBP substitutions.12,13 Our simulations suggest DOR takes a different route, anchoring instead to a feature the substitution cannot move. The hydrogen bond DOR accepts from the residue 103 main-chain carbonyl measures 2.97 ± 0.01 Å in WT and 3.05–3.20 Å in all four K103N-containing genotypes, even as the side-chain polar atoms at that position come roughly 3 Å closer to the drug (8.38 ± 0.15 Å in WT to 4.80–5.43 Å; Table 3). Binding is indifferent to what replaces the lysine because it never engaged the side chain in the first place — the structural expression of the design intent behind DOR.14,15 Equilibrium sampling on this timescale cannot address pocket-closure kinetics, but the preserved anchor may in part explain why K103N+M230L and L100I+K103N also show insignificant binding penalties (∆∆Gbind = 0.59 ± 0.41 and 0.19 ± 0.68 kcal/mol).

## Two changes beyond the restructuring

**A citation correction.** The current sentence attributes the closed-pocket
mechanism to "EFV and NVP" with ref 9. Hsiou et al. 2001 established that
mechanism using **loviride and HBY 097** structures, not EFV or NVP — the
Introduction already states this correctly. The replacement drops the drug names
from that clause and keeps EFV attached only to ref 8 (Schauer et al. 2014),
which does study efavirenz.

**A caveat that was missing.** The paragraph previously implied our simulations
speak to the entry-kinetics mechanism. They do not: 100 ns of equilibrium
sampling of an already-bound complex cannot report on pocket closure or
association rates. The replacement says so in one clause, which also keeps this
paragraph consistent with the Discussion's closing limitation.
