**R1.1, proposed line of answer.** Do not concede and hope. Decompose the
quantity the reviewer says is weakly correlated. ddG_bind as computed is the
difference between bound and unbound states of a pocket that is *already open*
in every starting structure, because every system inherits the DOR-bound 4NCG
geometry. So ddG_bind prices the interface and silently omits the work of
opening the pocket. With apo ensembles we can put a number on the omitted term
per genotype and show it is the same size as the spread in ddG_bind. That turns
"the correlation is weak" into "the correlation is weak *because* a term of
comparable magnitude is missing by construction, and here it is" — which is
both a stronger answer and the thing the reviewer actually asked about.

## The two questions you raised

### "Can we calculate MM/GBSA energies for just RT to get some insight?"

Yes, but not as an absolute number, and the framing matters.

**What will not work.** MM/GBSA energy of apo RT, compared across genotypes.
The genotypes differ in sequence, so the systems differ in atom count and atom
type, and the covalent baseline shifts by hundreds of kcal/mol between them.
A table of apo RT energies by genotype would not be comparable row to row.

**What does work, and answers R1.3 directly.** Within one genotype the atom
composition of the receptor is identical in the apo and the holo trajectory, so
the difference is well defined:

```
dE_reorg(g) = <E_RT>_from-holo-trajectory  -  <E_RT>_from-apo-trajectory
ddE_reorg(g) = dE_reorg(g) - dE_reorg(WT)
```

This is the receptor reorganization term, and it is precisely what the current
single-trajectory MM/GBSA cancels by construction: today the receptor is
extracted from the complex trajectory, so the strain of holding the pocket open
never enters ddE_Total. Computing it is the standard separate-trajectory
(three-trajectory) MM/GBSA. The reviewer's words in R1.3 are "whether such
remodeling could therefore be poorly reflected in ddG_bind" — ddE_reorg is the
quantity that question is about.

**The catch, stated up front.** Separate-trajectory MM/GBSA is notoriously
imprecise: E_RT for a system this size is on the order of -10^5 kcal/mol with
fluctuations of hundreds, and subtracting two independently sampled large
numbers inflates the error by one to two orders of magnitude over the
single-trajectory case. The raw whole-protein ddE_reorg will very likely have
error bars that swallow the signal.

Two ways to handle that, and I would do both:

1. **Restrict the subsystem.** Evaluate the reorganization energy over the
   NNIBP-lining residues plus a contact shell instead of all of RT. The
   invariant bulk cancels before it can contribute variance, and the result is
   interpretable as a pocket reorganization energy. This is the version likely
   to be publishable.
2. **Report the whole-protein number with its real error bar anyway.** If it is
   unresolvable, that is itself a legitimate and rather strong answer: the
   reorganization term cannot be resolved at this sampling, which is exactly why
   ddG_bind can miss a resistance mechanism that acts through it. An honest
   null is worth more here than a precise-looking number from a protocol that
   cannot support it.

Note this does **not** touch `ddg_full.csv` or any published ddE_Total. It is a
new, separately reported term.

### "What if we also ran just DOR simulations in explicit solvent?"

Worth doing, cheap, and it closes the one leg of the thermodynamic cycle we have
no data on. Recommended.

- **Cost.** One ligand in a small water box, a few thousand atoms. Minutes of
  GPU time for tens of ns. It does not need Sherlock.
- **DOR is identical in every genotype**, so a single solution ensemble is the
  common reference for all twenty. One short run buys a reference term for the
  whole panel.
- **What it gives.**
  - *Ligand strain on binding*: `dE_strain = <E_intra(DOR)>_bound - <E_intra(DOR)>_solution`.
    Unlike the receptor term this is a ~30-heavy-atom subsystem, so the variance
    is small and the number should actually resolve. This is the ligand-side
    counterpart to the reorganization term above, and it completes the cycle:
    interface + pocket opening + ligand strain.
  - *Conformational restriction*: we already have the four rotatable dihedrals
    tau1-tau4 for every holo replicate in `dor_torsions.csv`. The free-solution
    distributions turn those into a restriction measurement — how much of DOR's
    accessible torsional space the pocket removes, and whether genotypes with
    enlarged pockets (G190E, G190S) restrict it less. That is a per-genotype,
    torsion-level result, which is also what R2.3 asked for.
- **Caveat to state in Methods.** A solution ensemble from a few tens of ns
  samples the torsional basins but not necessarily their populations to
  convergence; report the dihedral distributions with block errors rather than
  single means.
